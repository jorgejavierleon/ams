# Rotating shift ("turnos rotativos") configuration in Talana, Buk, and GeoVictoria — competitor research for KOL-146

**Research question:** How do three Chilean HR/workforce-management competitors — Talana, Buk, and GeoVictoria — let an employer configure rotating shifts (4x4, 7x7, day/night alternation, and similar multi-day cycles)? Specifically: (1) does the admin UI offer a named preset cycle, a fully custom N-day cycle, a distinct "day/night alternation" concept, or a fully manual calendar with no repeating pattern; (2) what does the UI imply about the underlying data model — a repeating N-day pattern anchored to a start date (generative, projects forward indefinitely) vs. a calendar of hand-filled cells (no generative pattern); and (3) how do they handle multi-day cycles that don't divide evenly into 7 (e.g. an 8-day 4x4 cycle shifting which weekday the "on" block starts).

**Date of research:** 2026-10-06. **Method:** public sources only — official help-center articles, FAQ pages, marketing pages, and one public API PDF. No login/trial account was used or attempted for any of the three products; this document says plainly, per section, when something could not be confirmed for that reason. Claims are tagged as confirmed from a primary source (with a link) or flagged as this researcher's own inference.

A general access note: Talana's live help center (`ayuda.talana.com`) returned HTTP 403 with Cloudflare's bot-challenge headers (`cf-mitigated: challenge`) on every direct fetch attempt during this research (verified via `curl` with a standard browser user agent, and independently via a readability-proxy fetch) — this is a genuine bot-protection wall, not a missing/renamed page. To route around it without attempting to defeat the challenge, archived snapshots of the same official articles were pulled from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (`web.archive.org`), which mirrors the live page content as Google's crawler and other bots saw it. Those snapshots are cited below with their archive timestamps. Talana's marketing site (`web.talana.com`) and GeoVictoria's FAQ (`www.geovictoria.com`) were both reachable directly.

## Talana

**Sources used:** Wayback Machine snapshots of Talana's official Centro de Ayuda (help center), captured 2025-07/08/09, standing in for the live pages that are Cloudflare-blocked to automated fetches; plus the live marketing/FAQ page at `web.talana.com`.

### How the admin UI configures a rotating shift

Talana's shift module has (at least) a "Rotativo" shift type, created via a dedicated flow distinct from "Turnos semanales" and "Turnos mensuales". The defining field is **"Cantidad de días del ciclo"** ("define el número de días del ciclo. Por ejemplo: si el turno a crear es un 4x4, debes ingresar 8 días de ciclo. También, define qué días serán los que el trabajador debe marcar.") — [Turnos rotativos, Centro de Ayuda Talana (Wayback snapshot, 2025-08-10)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250810130003/https://ayuda.talana.com/hc/es-419/articles/7539920702867-Turnos-rotativos). This confirms:

- **No named preset ("4x4"/"7x7") exists as a dropdown or template type.** The admin always types an arbitrary cycle length in days; "4x4", "7x7", "4x3", "6x1" etc. are just worked examples in the documentation of what number to type (e.g. 8 for 4x4, 14 for 7x7), not selectable options. This is reinforced by Talana's own public FAQ: *"Sí, contamos con turnos rotativos que se crean mediante plantillas de turno. También tenemos la opción de crear esquemas como los 4x4 u otras combinaciones de jornada excepcional."* — [Software de asistencia de personal, web.talana.com FAQ](https://web.talana.com/software-de-asistencia-de-personal) (fetched live, 200 OK).
- **Day/night alternation is not a separate feature** — it's simply that different days-of-cycle within the same rotating shift can carry different schedules (including a night schedule), handled by the same "turnos rotativos" mechanism: *"Ciclo 4x4: Los empleados trabajan 4 días seguidos y luego tienen 4 días de descanso. Durante los 4 días de trabajo, pueden rotar entre diferentes turnos (día, tarde, noche)."* — [Recomendación de turnos por industria (Wayback snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://ayuda.talana.com/hc/es-419/articles/32564964533267-Recomendaci%C3%B3n-de-turnos-por-industria). Unlike Buk (below), the fetched Talana articles do not describe an "en escalera" (staggered day-1/day-2 entry-exit) mechanic for shifts crossing midnight — that specific wording was not found in any Talana source checked, so it is not claimed here; it may still exist in the live product but could not be verified publicly.
- **A fully manual, non-cyclical option also exists**, as an explicit escape hatch distinct from the generative rotating-cycle mechanism: the "por turno rotativo" Excel import template is described as being *"pensada particularmente para aquellos turnos que a pesar de ser rotativos no cumplen con un ciclo de días, sino que cada día de ciclo es un horario distinto"* ("for shifts that, despite being 'rotating', don't follow a day-cycle — each cycle day is simply a different, individually-specified schedule") — [Asignación por planilla (Excel) (Wayback snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://ayuda.talana.com/hc/es-419/articles/7540700743955-Asignaci%C3%B3n-por-planilla-Excel). This is Talana's answer to your category 4 (fully manual, hand-placed calendar, no underlying repeating rule) — it exists, but as a secondary/edge-case path, not the default rotating-shift experience.

### What the UI implies about the data model

Confirmed generative, start-date-anchored model, not a "fill in a calendar" model:

- Creating a rotating shift defines a template cycle (length + per-cycle-day schedule) once, independent of any calendar date.
- Assigning it to an employee requires choosing where in that cycle they start. Three separate archived help articles independently confirm this field exists (worded slightly differently across articles/versions, suggesting it may have been renamed over time or differs per assignment method):
  - "Asignación Masiva (Estándar)": *"Al momento de asignar un turno rotativo, el sistema habilitará un campo adicional que permite definir el día de inicio dentro del ciclo del turno... por ejemplo, día 1, día 2, etc."* — [Asignación Masiva (Estándar) (Wayback snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://ayuda.talana.com/hc/es-419/articles/7540062531347-Asignaci%C3%B3n-Masiva-Est%C3%A1ndar).
  - "Asignación Semanal (Calendario)": *"en el caso de que el turno sea de tipo rotativo, definir el día de partida del ciclo de acuerdo a las fechas."* — [Asignación Semanal (Calendario) (Wayback snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://ayuda.talana.com/hc/es-419/articles/7540101316243-Asignaci%C3%B3n-Semanal-Calendario).
  - A separate AI-generated web-search synthesis (not independently fetched by this researcher — see caveat below) used the field name **"posición inicial rotación"** with the same described behavior. This exact label could not be confirmed verbatim against any page this researcher directly retrieved; treat it as **unverified wording**, though the underlying field/behavior it describes matches the two confirmed quotes above closely enough to be the same feature.
- Once anchored, the cycle repeats automatically — the Excel-import worked example assigns *"un turno rotativo 4x4 de manera permanente... desde el 1 de junio"* with a "día de inicio" column, and explains that the system derives which day of the 8-day cycle any given date falls on (day 2 vs. day 3 of a new cycle, etc.) — [Asignación por planilla (Excel), same source as above]. This is exactly "repeating N-day pattern anchored to a start date, system computes day-of-cycle for any date," not a hand-filled calendar.

### Cycles not divisible by 7

Talana's own documentation states this distinction explicitly and generally, not just as an inference from field names: *"[turnos rotativos]... ser pactados por días de periodo, lo cual no hace referencia a días de la semana sino a días de ciclo"* ("...agreed in cycle-days, which does not refer to weekdays but to cycle-days") — [Recomendación de turnos por industria (Wayback snapshot)](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://ayuda.talana.com/hc/es-419/articles/32564964533267-Recomendaci%C3%B3n-de-turnos-por-industria). The same article gives a worked example with two workers on the same 7x7 (14-day cycle) shift definition, one starting "día 1" and the other "día 8" in the same calendar week, explicitly to produce an offset/staggered rotation. This directly confirms the mechanism behind your point 3 (a cycle's "day 1" is not pinned to a particular weekday — different employees or different cycle-start choices land "day 1" on different weekdays), though Talana's own text frames this as a deliberate staffing feature (staggered coverage) rather than as a caveat/quirk to manage.

## Buk

**Sources used:** Buk's live support center was directly reachable (no bot-wall encountered) — all citations below are a direct, confirmed fetch of the current live page, not an archive.

### How the admin UI configures a rotating shift

Buk's "Control de Asistencia" module has three shift types — **Permanente, Rotativo, Flexible** — selected from a "Crear" menu: *"En el módulo de Control de Asistencia puedes crear y asignar turnos (Permanentes, Rotativos y Flexibles)"* — [Gestión de turnos, Buk Centro de Ayuda Colombia](https://supportcenter.buk.co/hc/es-419/articles/41825147378075-Gesti%C3%B3n-de-turnos) (fetched live, 200 OK). For "Rotativo": *"se define como un turno donde se define la cantidad de días de un turno y que este se pueda asignar cíclicamente, de forma que se repita este patrón de turno"* (same source).

- **No preset dropdown for "4x4"/"7x7"/"10x10".** The creation flow asks the admin to *"Indica la cantidad de días que durará el turno, incluyendo los días de descanso. Por ejemplo, para un turno 7x7, se deben indicar 14 días de ciclo"* — a free-entry cycle-length field, with "7x7, 4x2, 10x10" used purely as illustrative examples in the article title and body, exactly as in Talana — [¿Cómo crear y asignar turnos rotativos, como 7x7, 4x2 o 10x10?, Buk Centro de Ayuda Colombia](https://supportcenter.buk.co/hc/es-419/articles/41571833141787--C%C3%B3mo-crear-y-asignar-turnos-rotativos-como-7x7-4x2-o-10x10) (fetched live, 200 OK; confirmed in-text: "No preset templates for 4x2, 7x7, or 10x10 are mentioned—all cycles are custom-configured").
- Each day-of-cycle gets its own "Hora ingreso"/"Hora salida" (rest days left blank), and a per-shift "Jornada" classification of Diurno vs. "Nocturno/Mixto" — so **day/night alternation is handled inside the same rotating-cycle mechanism**, not as a separate concept, exactly as the "Gestión de turnos" article states: *"Un colaborador puede tener asignado un horario diurno durante un período y posteriormente cambiar a un horario nocturno, según corresponda la rotación establecida."*
- For a shift that crosses midnight, Buk documents an explicit **"en escalera" (staggered)** data-entry convention: enter the start time on "Día 1" and the matching end time on "Día 2" of the cycle template, e.g. entry 20:00 on day 1, exit 05:00 on day 2, so *"el sistema interpreta correctamente que el colaborador ingresa el día 1 a las 20:00 y finaliza su jornada el día 2 a las 05:00"* (same source). This is a UI/data-entry convention for crossing midnight within a cycle template, not a distinct "night shift" feature type.
- **Rotating shifts cannot be created or assigned via the bulk Excel importers** — assignment must go through the dedicated "Turnos >> Asignación de turnos" screen: *"Este tipo de turno no puede ser asignado mediante Importadores"* (same source). This is a meaningful product limitation worth flagging: Buk's bulk-import path (unlike Talana's, which has a dedicated "por turno rotativo" template for fully manual/non-cyclical cases) does not support rotating shifts at all.

### What the UI implies about the data model

Confirmed generative, start-date-anchored model:

- Assignment requires selecting the shift, a **recurrence type** — *"Sin fecha de término" (repeats forever) or "Se repite con fecha de término" (repeats between given dates)* — and a field explicitly named **"Día que comienza a repetirse el ciclo."** The help article spells out the mechanism directly: *"El campo 'Día que comienza a repetirse el ciclo' permite definir desde qué día del ciclo comenzará la repetición. Por ejemplo, si el ciclo tiene una duración de 7 días y seleccionas 'Día 3', la repetición comenzará desde el tercer día, tomando como referencia la fecha de inicio del calendario."* — same source as above.
- "Sin fecha de término" repeating forever is explicit confirmation that the system projects the N-day pattern indefinitely forward from an anchor date, computing day-of-cycle for any future date — not a calendar the admin must keep refilling.
- One hard limitation confirmed in the same source and in ["Asigna Turnos Masivamente desde el Calendario"](https://supportcenter.buk.cl/hc/es-419/articles/41819730402843-Asigna-Turnos-Masivamente-desde-el-Calendario) (fetched live, 200 OK): rotating-shift assignment **only works for future dates** — *"Los turnos rotativos solo pueden asignarse hacia el futuro, desde el día siguiente a la fecha actual. No es posible realizar asignaciones retroactivas."*

### Cycles not divisible by 7

Buk's documentation describes the generic mechanism (cycle day is chosen independently, e.g. "Día 3" of a 7-day cycle) but — unlike Talana — **does not explicitly call out weekday drift for cycles that don't divide by 7** (e.g. an 8-day 4x4 cycle). None of the three Buk articles fetched contains language equivalent to Talana's "no hace referencia a días de la semana sino a días de ciclo." That the mechanism *would* produce the same weekday-drift effect (since "día de inicio del ciclo" is explicitly independent of weekday) is this researcher's own inference from the confirmed field behavior, not a direct Buk statement — flagged accordingly.

## GeoVictoria

**Sources used:** GeoVictoria's own public FAQ page (fetched live, 200 OK) was the strongest source found and is quoted directly below. GeoVictoria's dedicated help-center subdomain could not be located — `ayuda.geovictoria.com` and `soporte.geovictoria.com` both failed to connect (no HTTP response at all, not a 403/404), and a `site:` search against `wiki.geovictoria.com` (a subdomain that does resolve, hosting a public API PDF) returned nothing relevant to shift configuration. GeoVictoria's own blog posts on "turnos rotativos" ([example](https://www.geovictoria.com/es-cl/blog/recursos-humanos/turnos-rotativos-ejemplo/), fetched live) turned out to be general industry/legal-compliance content (citing Dirección del Trabajo rulings) rather than product documentation, and add essentially nothing to the UI/data-model questions beyond marketing claims. **This is the weakest-documented of the three competitors on this specific question** — most of what follows comes from one FAQ page, not a dedicated shift-configuration manual.

### How the admin UI configures a rotating shift

From the FAQ ([Preguntas Frecuentes, geovictoria.com](https://www.geovictoria.com/preguntas-frecuentes/), fetched live, 200 OK):

- Shift planning lives under **Planificaciones > Planificador**. The admin picks an existing planner from a list (each with an ID and name), sets a **"Período a planificar"** (date range the planner covers) and a **"Fecha Inicio Ciclo"** (cycle start date), then assigns users (by tree/search), and can mark the planner **"predeterminado"** ("default for the indicated users") so that it *"se cargará automáticamente para ese mismo usuario una vez que haya pasado el periodo planificado inicialmente"* (auto-reloads once the originally planned period has elapsed).
- For "turnos especiales" (the FAQ's own examples: **4x3, 14x7, 12x12**), the documented method is: *"Se puede realizar añadiendo días al planificador para generar una configuración de semana 'A' y semana 'B' por ejemplo 14 días y una vez se finalice el ciclo este se volverá a repetir siempre y cuando se haya establecido como predeterminado."* This is meaningfully different from Talana's/Buk's single "cantidad de días del ciclo" field: **there is no confirmed dedicated "cycle length" input** in GeoVictoria's flow. Instead, the admin manually builds out a "Week A / Week B"-style block inside the planner (described as adding individual days), and that whole built block is what repeats. **No named preset ("4x4" etc.) and no explicit custom-cycle-length field were found documented** — only this "build the block manually, then let the whole block repeat" pattern. This may undersell the real product (the help-center pages that would show the actual "Lista y Creación de Turnos" screen fields were not reachable publicly), so treat "no cycle-length field exists" as **not confirmed either way**, only that the FAQ never mentions one.
- Day/night alternation is not described as a separate concept; it's handled as part of the same planner. A night-shift-specific technical caveat is documented, though: an hourly/"por hora" shift type is described as incompatible with jornada nocturna tracking, and night-shift marks can land on the following calendar day — *"el turno por hora que se está utilizando actualmente no es compatible con la jornada nocturna, por esta razón la marca aparece al otro día"* (same FAQ source). A real-world user question in the same FAQ references a genuine 4x4 20:00–08:00 night shift, confirming the pattern is in active use by customers, though without full configuration-screen detail.

### What the UI implies about the data model

This is where GeoVictoria's FAQ gives the single strongest, most explicit statement found across all three competitors about the underlying mechanism — and it points to a **hybrid** model, not cleanly "generative" nor cleanly "hand-filled calendar":

- Explicit generative/anchored-numbering confirmation: *"la plataforma no entiende de días de semana, solo ve una numeración corrida, entonces hay que especificar que, si bien el trabajador parte sus funciones a mitad de semana, el turno ha avanzado, así calzarán las fechas con lo que necesitas planificar y no quedará desfasado."* ("The platform doesn't understand weekdays, it only sees a running/continuous day-count...") — same FAQ source, in answer to a question about a worker whose contract starts mid-week. This is a direct, explicit primary-source statement that the system tracks a continuous day-index from the cycle start date, not calendar weekdays — the defining trait of a generative pattern.
- However, nothing in the FAQ describes an abstract "N days on / M days off" rule being entered once and generated automatically; the "Week A / Week B" guidance above implies the admin manually lays out each day's assignment within the planned period, and what repeats is that whole authored block (once marked "predeterminado"), not a re-derived pattern from a short "4 on, 4 off" rule. **This split (anchored day-numbering + manually-authored day content, repeated as a block) is this researcher's own synthesis of the FAQ's wording, not a single explicit GeoVictoria statement** — it is offered as the best reading of the available public text, flagged clearly as inference.
- GeoVictoria's public API documentation ([API-GV3.pdf, wiki.geovictoria.com](https://wiki.geovictoria.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/API-GV3.pdf), fetched live) exposes assigned shifts per day with `Begins`/`Ends`/`ShiftDisplay` fields in the attendance-book read endpoint, consistent with a system that resolves "what shift applies on date X" to a flat answer for reporting — but this PDF documents read/reporting endpoints, not the shift-creation/planning admin screens, so it doesn't settle the authoring-model question either way.

### Cycles not divisible by 7

This is the one point where GeoVictoria's FAQ is the **most explicit and most repeated** of all three competitors, with multiple worked examples, though framed around weekly (7-day) alignment rather than an 8-day 4x4 cycle specifically:

- *"Si tus turnos parten un lunes, procura que en el campo 'Fecha Inicio Ciclo' esté un lunes seleccionado. Por ejemplo, si tu periodo a planificar es del 1 de mayo al 30 de junio y 1 de mayo cae jueves, en el campo Fecha Inicio Ciclo, selecciona el lunes previo al periodo a planificar. De esta manera, no tendrás un problema de desfase en los días."*
- *"Cuando creo un planificador y mi semana laboral inicia un día lunes debo fijarme que en el 'periodo a planificar' y en 'fecha de inicio de ciclo' este siempre inicie un lunes... si inicio un miércoles los días de descanso no me van a coincidir con el fin de semana."*
- *"...recuerda que en el campo 'Fecha Inicio Ciclo', debes elegir el lunes previo al periodo a planificar. ¿Por qué? Porque la plataforma no entiende de días de semana, solo ve una numeración corrida..."*

All three quotes are from the same [Preguntas Frecuentes](https://www.geovictoria.com/preguntas-frecuentes/) page. These confirm that misalignment between a cycle's internal day-count and the calendar week is a known, FAQ-worthy support burden for GeoVictoria customers, and that the documented workaround is entirely manual (the admin must pick the correct Monday by hand every time), with no mention of the system doing this automatically. No example specific to an 8-day (4x4) cycle's weekday drift was found, but the underlying "numeración corrida" mechanism described would produce exactly that effect — stated here as inference, not a direct GeoVictoria quote about 4x4 specifically.
